### NotebookLM Suggestions — The MIT Method for Learning Any Subject in 90 Minutes

The idea isn't to ask for summaries.

The idea is to build context, identify what's important, test your knowledge, correct your mistakes, and create a review plan.

You can use these suggestions with any subject.

### How to Use These Instructions

1. Create a notebook in NotebookLM on the topic you want to learn.
2. Load various sources: articles, videos, PDFs, academic papers, notes, or transcripts.
3. Start with the basic concepts topic.
4. Continue with the teaching topic.
5. Develop challenging questions.
6. Answer them without consulting the sources.
7. Ask for feedback.
8. Create a 90-minute study session.

9. Finish with a review kit to consolidate your learning.

The key is not that NotebookLM gives you the answers. The key is to use it to ask better questions, identify your mistakes, and study more actively.

# 1. Foundation and Basic Intuition

Act as my Socratic Tutor, a Didactics Specialist. I am beginning to study the topic: **[INSERT TOPIC]**.

Before we delve into technical details, complexity, or depth, I need to build a solid and simple intuition. Please answer strictly following this structure:

1. **The Big Idea (Analogy)**: Explain the central concept as if I were 12 years old, using only one real-world analogy. Zero technical jargon.

2. **Survival Vocabulary**: List a maximum of 3 to 5 absolutely essential technical terms that I need to know so I don't get lost in my readings, with one-sentence definitions each.

3. **The Problem It Solves (and What It Is NOT)**: What is the "pain point," chaos, or inefficiency that existed before this solution? Why did anyone bother to create this? And, in a short sentence, what is this concept not (to avoid common beginner confusions)?
4. **Roots and Pioneers**:
* Etymology: What is the origin of the word or term (if relevant and interesting)?
* Brief Timeline: A timeline of 2 to 3 key milestones in how this idea evolved.

* Key Thinkers: 1 or 2 names of pioneers, scientists, or engineers who shaped this concept and what their central contribution was.

4. **Socratic Intuition Question**: Ask me a simple conceptual question, based on the analogy in item 1 or the problem in item 3, to check if I've grasped the essence. Don't require technical knowledge in the answer.
5. **Ready Signal**: Tell me what the practical 'signal' is that I understand the basics and am ready to move on to the in-depth prompts (like the Essential Concepts one) in my Study Guide.

* **Golden Rule**: Do not move on to trade-offs, complex architecture, or implementation details in this answer. "Keep your focus 100% on pure intuition and purpose."

# 2. Essential Subject Concepts

Act as my Socratic Expert Tutor and Senior Examiner. From now on, we will study the following subject: [TOPIC]
Based on ALL the sources in this notebook—articles, academic papers, videos, guides, PDFs, and materials on [TOPIC]—identify the 5 essential concepts to understand about [TOPIC].

I don't want a summary of each source. I want the **MAIN IDEAS** that underpin the entire topic: the principles that explain how [TOPIC] really works, which an expert would consider obvious, but which a beginner would normally not be able to connect.

For each main concept:

1. Name it with a clear sentence.
2. Explain why it is important for better learning.
3. Give a practical example of how to apply it when studying, working, or learning a new skill.
4. Indicate which other concepts present it relates to. Relates.
5. Explain it in the following way:
Feynman Explanation (Level Progression):
Basic: Explain as if you were telling a 12-year-old child, without jargon, necessarily using a real-world analogy.
Intermediate: Introduce the correct technical terms and explain the standard operating mechanism.
Advanced: Detail the nuances, trade-offs (costs, complexity, scalability) and how this applies to real-world systems or contexts in the field of study.
Expert/Master: Point out edge cases, hidden limitations, pitfalls of practical application, or current debates in the literature on this concept.

6. Point out the typical mistake someone makes when they don't fully understand this concept.

7. Cite at least two sources from your notebook that support you.
Prioritize concepts that appear repeatedly in various sources and that help build a comprehensive mind map of the topic.

# 3. What do I need to understand to teach this?

This question helps you move from "I I recognize the concept" to "I can explain it from scratch."

What do I really need to understand about this material to be able to teach it to someone who knows nothing about [TOPIC]?
I don't want a superficial explanation. I want you to identify the key ideas.

You need to master what you need to explain clearly from scratch.

For each idea, tell me:

1. What do I really need to understand?

2. What is often confused or poorly explained?

3. What simple example could I use? Or what simple analogy could I use?

4. What would indicate that I still don't fully understand?

5. What resources in the notebook should I review to deepen my understanding of the subject?

Prioritize what is most important to understand, master, and teach the topic clearly, practically, and coherently.

# 4. Questions that reveal if you only memorized
This instruction transforms the notebook into a deep comprehension test.

Generate 5 questions that reveal if someone truly understands [TOPIC] correctly or if they only memorized isolated clues.

The questions should:

1. Require reasoning, not memorization.
2. Connect concepts from various sources in the notebook.
3. Include real-life study or work situations.
4. Show examples where typical advice might fail.
5. Be ordered from easiest to most difficult.

My answer:

2. Which part is superficial?
3. Which concept did I not fully understand?
4. What specific sources should I consult?
5. How would a subject matter expert have answered?
6. Ask me a follow-up question to test if I corrected my gap.
